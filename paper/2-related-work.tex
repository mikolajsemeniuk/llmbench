\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Reference-based metrics.} The oldest family compares a candidate with one or more human references. Lexical metrics count shared $n$-grams (BLEU~\citep{papineni2002bleu}, ROUGE~\citep{lin2004rouge}, METEOR~\citep{banerjee2005meteor}) or characters (chrF~\citep{popovic2015chrf}); SMART~\citep{amplayo2022smart} matches sentences instead of tokens. Embedding metrics replace exact matches by similarity in a contextual embedding space: BERTScore~\citep{zhang2020bertscore} aligns tokens greedily and MoverScore~\citep{zhao2019moverscore} solves a transport problem. Trained metrics such as BLEURT~\citep{sellam2020bleurt} and COMET~\citep{rei2020comet} fine-tune an encoder on human judgements. All of them need a reference, which is the resource that is missing whenever summaries are produced at scale.

\paragraph{Reference-free metrics before LLM judges.} Several metrics compare the summary with the source instead. SUPERT~\citep{gao2020supert} builds a pseudo-reference from salient source sentences and scores the summary against it with contextual embeddings. BLANC~\citep{vasilyev2020blanc} measures how much a summary helps a masked language model reconstruct masked source tokens. SummaQA~\citep{scialom2019summaqa} asks questions generated from the source and checks whether a QA model can answer them from the summary; QAFactEval~\citep{fabbri2022qafacteval} refines this idea for factual consistency. ESTIME~\citep{vasilyev2021estime} counts summary tokens whose contextual embeddings are mismatched in the source. These methods are cheap and need no reference, but most of them reward overlap with the source by construction.

\paragraph{Likelihood-based scoring.} A language model can score a summary by its probability. BARTScore~\citep{yuan2021bartscore} uses a sequence-to-sequence model in several directions, including source to summary; GPTScore~\citep{fu2023gptscore} prompts a causal model with an instruction. FFLM~\citep{jia2023fflm} combines probability changes with and without the source to judge faithfulness. Prism~\citep{thompson2020prism} already noted that a paraphraser scores a verbatim copy of its input highly. Perturbation contrasts compare the probability of a text with that of perturbed versions: DeltaScore~\citep{xie2023deltascore} uses a single perturbation per aspect for story evaluation, and DetectGPT~\citep{mitchell2023detectgpt} averages over many perturbations to detect machine-generated text. LNC reuses these ideas as features; it does not introduce a new likelihood primitive.

\paragraph{Factual consistency.} Inconsistency with the source is the best studied error of abstractive summarisers~\citep{maynez2020faithfulness}. FactCC~\citep{kryscinski2020factcc} trains a classifier on synthetic errors. SummaC~\citep{laban2022summac} applies an off-the-shelf NLI model to every pair of source and summary sentences and aggregates the entailment scores; its zero-shot variant is one of LNC's components. AlignScore~\citep{zha2023alignscore} trains a unified alignment model on many tasks and is a strong published baseline for consistency, which is why we include it. MiniCheck~\citep{tang2024minicheck} trains small fact-checkers for grounding documents. FRANK~\citep{pagnoni2021frank} provides human factuality annotations with an error typology and is one of our transfer corpora.

\paragraph{Unified evaluators and LLM judges.} UniEval~\citep{zhong2022unieval} casts every dimension as a Boolean question answered by a T5 model trained on pseudo-labelled data. G-Eval~\citep{liu2023geval} asks GPT-4 for a score with chain-of-thought instructions and averages twenty samples; the broader use of LLMs as judges is surveyed by~\citet{gu2024judgesurvey}. These evaluators correlate best with human ratings, but the strongest configurations depend on proprietary models or need a reference for some dimensions.

\paragraph{Learned metric combinations.} MetaMetrics~\citep{winata2025metametrics} calibrates a combination of existing metrics to human preferences and reports runtime next to correlation. LNC belongs to this family: it is a learned combination calibrated on human ratings. It differs in that its inputs are signals from two small models rather than complete metrics, it is designed and assessed on the shortcut-controlled axis, and its calibration is frozen and tested on corpora it was not fitted on.

\paragraph{Meta-evaluation methodology.} \citet{deutsch2021resampling} show that correlations on summarisation benchmarks carry wide confidence intervals and recommend resampling tests, which we use throughout. \citet{gehrmann2023cracked} survey wider problems of evaluation practice. Closest to our motivation, \citet{durmus2022spurious} show that reference-free factuality metrics are confounded with extractiveness and \citet{ladhak2022faithful} describe the trade-off between faithfulness and abstractiveness. The lead bias of news summarisation~\citep{kedzie2018content} and the extractive strategies measured by Newsroom~\citep{grusky2018newsroom} explain why the confound is so strong on CNN/DailyMail. We extend the confound analysis from consistency to all four SummEval dimensions and to the whole metric pool. RoSE~\citep{liu2023rose} replaces Likert ratings by atomic content units and is our second transfer corpus.
