\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Evaluating summaries at scale needs metrics that work without human references and without a call to a large language model for every summary. We propose LNC, a reference-free Likelihood--NLI Composite that scores coherence, consistency, fluency and relevance from two small open models: ten signals from a 1.5B-parameter causal language model and a DeBERTa entailment model, including source-conditioned likelihood, contrasts against minimally perturbed versions of the summary and sentence-level entailment, combined by a per-dimension ridge calibration fitted on 50 SummEval articles. To show that LNC measures more than surface overlap, we evaluate it under a protocol that controls for the extractive shortcut of summarisation benchmarks: on SummEval, a bare bigram copy rate already correlates with human ratings better than ten of the fourteen established metrics we evaluate, so we report every correlation both raw and with the copy rate partialled out, and time every metric on the same machine. On held-out SummEval articles LNC reaches a partial correlation of .350 at 216\,ms per summary, above every other reference-free metric, including a local LLM judge (.291) that costs 82 times more, and it lies on the cost--quality Pareto frontier on both axes; only UniEval, which needs a reference for relevance, is ahead (.424). A pre-registered transfer test with the calibration frozen shows that LNC's consistency judgement generalises: on FRANK CNN/DM it outperforms UniEval and the LLM judge and matches the specialised AlignScore, and on the abstractive FRANK XSum it ties all three. Its relevance judgement does not transfer to RoSE's content-unit annotations, and controls show that part of the calibration is specific to the systems it was fitted on. Negative results with three alternative designs explain why signals that merely cancel surface overlap lose useful information, and motivate combining likelihood with entailment instead.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} summarisation evaluation; reference-free metrics; natural language inference; language-model likelihood; extractive shortcut; meta-evaluation; pre-registration
