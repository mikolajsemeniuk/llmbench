\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Evaluating summaries at scale needs metrics that work without human references and without a call to a large language model for every summary. We propose LNC, a reference-free Likelihood--NLI Composite that scores coherence, consistency, fluency and relevance from two small open models: ten signals from a 1.5B-parameter causal language model and a DeBERTa entailment model, including source-conditioned likelihood, contrasts against minimally perturbed versions of the summary and sentence-level entailment, combined by a per-dimension ridge calibration fitted on 50 SummEval articles. To show that LNC measures more than surface overlap, we evaluate it under a protocol that controls for the extractive shortcut of summarisation benchmarks: on SummEval, a bare bigram copy rate already correlates with human ratings better than ten of the fourteen established metrics we evaluate, so we report every correlation both raw and with the copy rate partialled out, and time every metric on the same machine. On held-out SummEval articles LNC reaches a partial correlation of .350 at 216\,ms per summary, above every other reference-free metric, including a local LLM judge (.291) that costs 82 times more, and it lies on the cost--quality Pareto frontier on both axes; only UniEval, which needs a reference for relevance, is ahead (.424). We then freeze the calibration and put it through a transfer test whose success criterion was pre-registered, and which, as written, requires LNC to improve significantly on its own strongest component; that criterion is not met, and we report the negative verdict as stated rather than relaxing it after the fact. What the test resolves to is more useful than a verdict: the consistency scorer reaches the highest correlation of any metric we run on FRANK CNN/DM, above UniEval and the LLM judge and level with the specialised AlignScore, and ties all three on the abstractive FRANK XSum, whereas the relevance scorer does not carry over to RoSE's content-unit annotations, where a lead-sentence heuristic does as well. Calibrated controls bound the in-domain claim with the same discipline: the better estimate of LNC's accuracy is .322 over random splits, part of the calibration is specific to the systems it was fitted on, and a ridge fitted on existing metrics reaches that same .322 --- but only by reading a human reference and at five times the cost. Negative results with three alternative designs explain why signals that merely cancel surface overlap lose useful information, and motivate combining likelihood with entailment instead.
\end{abstract}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} summarisation evaluation; reference-free metrics; natural language inference; language-model likelihood; extractive shortcut; meta-evaluation; pre-registration
