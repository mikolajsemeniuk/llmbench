0. THE SITUATION IN ONE PARAGRAPH
================================================================================

The paper claimed LGS is Pareto-optimal in a cost-quality plane. We built the
trivial control a reviewer asked for -- keep the first k source sentences, match
with ROUGE-L, no model at all -- and it MATCHES OR BEATS LGS on the paper's own
axis at ~1000x lower cost. That kills the Pareto claim as stated. Investigating
why produced something better: SummEval's human ratings are heavily confounded
with extractiveness (how much the summary copies verbatim from the source), and
a bare n-gram copy counter outscores 11 of the 14 metrics in the paper's pool.
Controlling for that confound, LGS recovers relative to the lead baselines but
is NOT statistically distinguishable from them. Recommendation is to pivot the
paper from "we built a metric" to "we found a confound in how this whole field
is measured" -- see section 6.

2.1  lambda* IS NOT IDENTIFIABLE   (tool: cmd/lambdaci, make paper-lambdaci)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Method: instead of reporting the argmax of the lambda sweep as a number, re-run
the ENTIRE selection procedure inside the cluster bootstrap. On each of 5,000
resamples of the 50 development articles, recompute mean Spearman rho at every
grid value and record which value wins. That gives a distribution over the grid
instead of a single winner.

Selection mass, dev split, nomic-embed-text:

    lambda    0     0.25   0.3    0.4    0.5    0.6    0.75   1.0    2.0
    P(win)  0.7%   17.5%  27.6%  20.4%  17.8%   9.9%   2.6%   2.9%   0.5%

TWO CONCLUSIONS, POINTING OPPOSITE WAYS:

  (a) SWITCHING THE PRIOR ON IS ROBUST. lambda=0 wins 0.7% of dev resamples and
      0.3% of test resamples. "Some positional prior beats none" survives
      resampling in >99% of draws. This claim is safe.

  (b) WHICH EXPONENT IS NOT IDENTIFIABLE. The reported lambda*=0.5 wins 17.8%.
      Four grid values sit within 10 points of each other. The protocol returns
      the INTERVAL [0.25, 0.6], not a point. 50 articles cannot separate them.

Also found: on the combined 9-point grid the dev argmax is actually lambda=0.4
(.2528) not 0.5 (.2519). The paper's 0.5 came from the coarse 5-point grid
selected before the finer sweep was added, so the protocol is internally
consistent, but this was disclosed in the text.

PAIRED CONTROLS (all on the grid common to every configuration, {0,.25,.5,1,2};
comparing agreement rates across grids of different size is invalid, the
probability of two argmaxes coinciding falls mechanically as the grid grows):

  comparison                                     P(same)  P(A<B)  P(A>B)
  cross-backbone, shared draw (nomic vs bge-m3)   37.8%    0.4%   61.9%
  SAME config, two independent draws (NOISE FLOOR) 43.5%   28.6%  27.9%
  dev vs test, same backbone                      46.3%    0.9%   52.8%

How to read this:
  - Agreement rate alone proves NOTHING. Two different backbones agree 37.8% of
    the time; one unchanged backbone agrees with ITSELF 43.5% of the time.
    Nearly identical.
  - DIRECTION does carry signal. The noise floor is symmetric by construction
    (28.6 vs 27.9, a coin flip). Cross-backbone is 61.9 vs 0.4, a ~155:1 ratio
    that no symmetric noise process produces. So bge-m3 really does prefer a
    weaker prior.
  - BUT the same asymmetry appears when only the ARTICLES change under a fixed
    backbone (52.8 vs 0.9). So the backbone is NOT isolated as the cause. The
    honest claim is "re-derive the exponent in situ", not "the backbone is the
    variable that invalidates a borrowed value".

Paper was updated accordingly in commit 59e9c2c (new section 7.3
"How identifiable is lambda*?", plus abstract, contribution 4, 4.1, 4.3, 7.2,
7.4, 8.2, conclusion).

2.3  THE LEAD-WINDOW CONTROL -- THIS IS THE BAD ONE
     (tool: cmd/leadbaseline, make benchmark-lead / benchmark-lead-sweep)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built the control reviewer #5 asked for. Two modes:
  whole : ROUGE-L(first k source sentences as one block, candidate as one block)
  sent  : mean over candidate sentences of max ROUGE-L against any of the first
          k source sentences -- i.e. LGS's OWN mean-of-max aggregation, with
          lexical overlap instead of embedding cosine and a hard cutoff instead
          of the exponential prior.
Both are reference-free like LGS, so the comparison isolates the machinery.

RAW mean Spearman rho (the axis the paper's whole Pareto argument uses):

    metric              ms/sample    mean rho
    Lead-5 (sent)          0.06        .296     <-- beats LGS
    Lead-3 (sent)          0.04        .284     <-- ties LGS
    Lead-3 (whole)         0.02        .267
    LGS                   57.01        .284
    EmbedScorer          339.33        .174
    BERTScore            567.66        .250
    UniEval              430.31        .470

Lead-5 is 950x cheaper than LGS and correlates BETTER. LGS is therefore
DOMINATED and the Pareto-optimality claim -- the paper's headline contribution
-- is false once this baseline is in the pool.

Per-dimension the two are complementary (paired bootstrap, 5000 resamples):
    LGS beats Lead-5 on coherence  +.105  and relevance +.079  (significant)
    Lead-5 beats LGS on consistency -.129 and fluency   -.103  (significant)
The MEAN is what the Pareto argument uses, and the mean is lost.

2.4  WHY LEAD-K WINS: THE EXTRACTIVENESS CONFOUND  (NEW FINDING)
     (tool: cmd/confound, make paper-confound)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Hypothesis tested: lead-k is not measuring quality, it is measuring how much the
summary COPIES from the source, and SummEval's human ratings reward copying.

Defined copy rate = fraction of the candidate's token bigrams that occur
verbatim in the source. No model, no reference, no hyperparameter. Pure counting.

COPY RATE ALONE, scored as if it were a metric:

    dimension     coherence  consistency  fluency  relevance   MEAN
    rho             .210        .382        .305      .203      .275

Mean rho .275 -- that OUTRANKS 11 OF THE 14 METRICS in the paper's own pool
(BERTScore .250, GPTScore .264, ChrF .184, ROUGE .153, METEOR .162, BLEU .128,
BARTScore .166, MoverScore .060, EmbedScorer .174, SMART-Model .202,
SMART-String .084). A bigram counter beats most of the published literature on
this benchmark's headline axis.

How much of a copy detector each metric is (Spearman with copy rate):
    Lead-5 .684 | Lead-3 .607 | GPTScore .433 | G-Eval .404 | UniEval .396
    LGS .386 | ChrF .232 | BLEU .149 | BARTScore .128 | BERTScore .058

BERTScore is nearly unconfounded because it is reference-based -- it never sees
the source, so extractiveness cannot leak in. That is the mechanism.

PARTIAL correlation (copy rate partialled out of both sides), cluster-bootstrap
CI over articles, full set:

    metric        raw    partial    drop     rho_copy   ms/sample
    UniEval      .470     .424      .046       .396      430.31
    G-Eval       .359     .318      .042       .404    13593.99
    BERTScore    .250     .241      .008       .058      567.66
    LGS          .284     .198      .085       .386       57.01
    SMART-Model  .202     .182      .020       .096      890.02
    GPTScore     .264     .165      .099       .433       90.53
    METEOR       .162     .165     -.003       .013        0.98
    EmbedScorer  .174     .164      .010       .052      339.33
    Lead-5       .296     .154      .142       .684        0.06
    Lead-3       .284     .154      .131       .607        0.04
    ChrF         .184     .128      .056       .232        2.77
    ROUGE-L      .153     .127      .026       .115        0.12
    copy rate    .275     .000      .275      1.000        0.00

LGS 95% CI on the partial axis: [.158, .238]. Lead-5: [.115, .192].

PAIRED tests on the partial axis (5000 resamples, delta = LGS minus baseline):
    vs Lead-5     +.044   [+.004, +.084]   p = .033   marginal

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5  HEADROOM: THE CHEAP REGIME IS SATURATED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Question: can a better cheap metric be built by combining existing cheap signals?
Evaluated on the HELD-OUT TEST HALF (last 50 articles) only, rank-averaged
combinations:

    combination           raw rho   partial rho   rho_copy    ms
    LGS alone               .319       .230         .433     57.01
    Lead-5 alone            .292       .147         .708      0.06
    copy rate alone         .271       .000        1.000      0.00
    LGS + Lead-5            .334       .219         .623     57.07
    LGS + copy rate         .350       .233         .840     57.01
    LGS + Lead-5 + ChrF     .339       .230         .583     59.84
    all four                .348       .225         .778     59.84
    UniEval (reference)     .473       .424           -     430.31

Combining raises RAW rho (up to .350) purely by adding more copy detection
(rho_copy climbs to .84) and changes the partial axis by nothing. Everything
built from source-similarity -- lexical, positional, or embedding -- plateaus
at partial rho ~= .23.

CONCLUSION: "a better LGS" does not exist. Any metric of this signal class hits
the same ceiling. Breaking past .23 cheaply requires a DIFFERENT KIND of signal,
not better weighting of this one. UniEval sits at .424 because it performs a
learned discriminative judgement, not a similarity measurement.

6. STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATION
================================================================================

KBS imposed a 6-month embargo and requires >=50% difference for resubmission as
a new submission. So a light revision is not an option anyway; something has to
change structurally. Also consider whether KBS is the right venue at all -- the
reviewer pool that produced a knowledge-graph citation demand and a
misfiled ABSA review looks topically distant. IPM, Natural Language Engineering,
Computer Speech & Language, or ACL/EMNLP are closer.

THE CORE PROBLEM, stated plainly: the component the paper is named after (the
exponential lead-bias prior) contributes +.005 held-out and its exponent is not
identifiable; the component that does the work (sentence-level reference-free
grounding) is a recombination of known parts whose closest prior art is not
cited; and the whole thing is now beaten on the paper's own axis by a four-line
lexical heuristic. The metric cannot carry a paper.

WHAT CAN: the confound finding in 2.4. It is complete, reproducible from stored
data, hardware-independent, and it explains the behaviour of the entire metric
pool rather than one metric. A bare bigram counter outscoring 11 of 14 published
metrics on this benchmark's headline axis is a result about how the field
measures things, and no reviewer criticism in section 1 touches it -- every one
of them attacks claims about LGS, and this paper would make none.

PROPOSED SPLIT:

  PAPER 1 (mostly written already, in data terms):
    "Mean correlation on SummEval largely measures extractiveness."
    - 17 metrics under one controlled budget
    - the copy-rate diagnostic and the partial-correlation axis
    - lead-window controls
    - rankings recomputed on the corrected axis
    - LGS included honestly as one data point, including the fact that it does
      not separate from Lead-3
    - the lambda-identifiability methodology from 2.1 generalises: submit the
      SELECTION PROCEDURE to the bootstrap, not just the final number

  PAPER 2 (new work, motivated by paper 1):
    A metric designed against the corrected axis. Design target, now measurable:
    partial rho > .23 at cost far below UniEval's 430 ms, with low rho_copy.
    Candidate signals, chosen because they are structurally NOT copy detectors:
      consistency <- NLI / entailment (entailment holds for paraphrase, so it
                     does not reward verbatim copying). Also directly answers
                     reviewers #4 and #6 on "cosine is not entailment".
      fluency     <- small-LM perplexity (never looks at the source at all)
      coherence   <- discourse features: entity grid, sentence ordering
      relevance   <- source grounding (the one place LGS actually works)

  GO/NO-GO FOR PAPER 2, run this before committing months:
    Take one small NLI model on your GX10, score (source sentence, summary
    sentence) pairs, and compute the CONSISTENCY partial rho. If it clears .23,
    the signal class is right and the rest is engineering. If it plateaus at the
    same ceiling, the problem is not the signal type and the design needs
